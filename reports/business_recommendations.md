# Business Recommendations

## Recommendation 1: Use Regional HGV Traffic as a Logistics Demand Indicator

HGV traffic can be used as a useful indicator of freight and logistics demand across UK regions.

### Suggested action

Transport and logistics teams should monitor HGV traffic by region to identify areas with higher freight movement and potential network pressure.

This can support:

- transport planning
- route planning
- depot location analysis
- regional capacity review
- operational demand forecasting

---

## Recommendation 2: Monitor LGV Growth as an E-commerce and Last-Mile Indicator

LGV traffic is highly relevant to parcel, e-commerce, courier and last-mile delivery activity.

### Suggested action

Businesses should monitor LGV traffic trends by region to understand where delivery activity and road demand are increasing.

This can support decisions around:

- local delivery capacity
- driver planning
- van fleet planning
- urban logistics operations
- regional service demand

---

## Recommendation 3: Use Traffic per KM as a Logistics Pressure Proxy

Traffic per kilometre helps compare activity relative to road network length.

A region with high HGV or LGV traffic per kilometre may experience greater transport pressure than a region with similar traffic volume but a larger road network.

### Suggested action

Use HGVs per KM and LGVs per KM as logistics pressure indicators when reviewing regional transport demand.

This can help identify areas where traffic intensity may affect:

- journey time reliability
- route planning
- delivery performance
- congestion exposure
- fleet utilisation

---

## Recommendation 4: Review Road Category Distribution for Network Planning

Road category distribution helps show the structure of the regional road network.

Motorways, trunk roads and principal roads support different types of freight and logistics movement.

### Suggested action

Use road category analysis to understand how regional road infrastructure may support or constrain logistics activity.

This can support:

- trunking route planning
- regional network design
- site location decisions
- road capacity discussions
- resilience planning

---

## Recommendation 5: Combine Public Transport Data with Internal Operational Data

Public road traffic data becomes more powerful when combined with internal business data.

### Suggested action

A logistics operator could combine this dashboard with internal data such as:

- delivery volumes
- route performance
- driver hours
- vehicle utilisation
- fuel cost
- failed deliveries
- depot throughput
- trailer movements
- customer demand

This would allow the business to compare external road pressure with internal operational performance.

---

## Recommendation 6: Build a Regional Logistics Pressure Score

A future improvement would be to create a single regional logistics pressure score.

### Suggested action

Create a scoring model using:

- HGV traffic
- LGV traffic
- total motor traffic
- HGV traffic per KM
- LGV traffic per KM
- road network length
- road category mix
- long-term traffic growth

This score could help rank regions by logistics pressure and support senior management decision-making.

---

## Recommendation 7: Add Freight Volume and Empty Running Data

The current dashboard focuses mainly on regional traffic and road network data.

### Suggested action

Future versions should include more detailed freight statistics, such as:

- goods lifted
- goods moved
- tonne kilometres
- empty running
- loading factor
- origin and destination flows

This would strengthen the connection between road traffic activity and freight productivity.

---

## Summary

This project shows how public UK transport datasets can be converted into a practical logistics intelligence dashboard.

The key business value is the ability to compare regional HGV activity, LGV activity, traffic pressure and road network structure in one place.

For a logistics, transport or operations team, this type of dashboard can support better planning, network review and evidence-based decision-making.
